# HackathonFinder.in — Market and Product Brief

**Prepared:** 25 October 2026  
**Purpose:** A clear product, market, competitor, positioning, and execution brief for pitches and planning.

## The idea in one sentence

HackathonFinder.in brings hackathons and similar build competitions from many websites and community sources into one searchable, India-aware place, so students can find a relevant event before its deadline and register with the official organizer.

## A short pitch you can say

> Students find hackathons across Unstop, Devfolio, Devpost, college websites, social posts, and community groups. Because the information is scattered, they miss events, spend hours searching, or discover them after registration closes. HackathonFinder collects those opportunities in one place, checks and standardizes the key details, and lets students filter by city, online or in-person mode, eligibility, team size, theme, fee, and deadline. Students still register with the official organizer. We are starting with India, where nearby offline events and college-level announcements matter, then expanding coverage as our listing quality grows.

**Short tagline:** Every hackathon. One place.

## The problem

### What students struggle with

- **Discovery is fragmented.** Events are spread across event platforms, college pages, social posts, and student communities. No single host can show every event.
- **The search is manual.** Students repeatedly check several sites and feeds, then compare dates, eligibility, team rules, location, and cost themselves.
- **Good opportunities are easy to miss.** Events may be shared through a college club or local post and never reach a student's usual platform.
- **The details are inconsistent.** Each source presents event dates, deadlines, location, team size, prizes, and eligibility differently.
- **Local fit matters.** A student may need an affordable in-person event nearby, or may only be able to join online. Many event pages do not make that comparison easy.
- **Finding an event is only the first step.** Students may still need a teammate, a deadline reminder, and a way to track which events they entered.

### Job to be done

> When I want to build something and gain practical experience, help me find trustworthy, open events that fit my interests, eligibility, team, schedule, and location, and make it easy to act before the deadline.

## The solution

HackathonFinder is a **discovery and decision layer**, not an event-hosting or registration platform.

1. **Collect:** find event listings from established platforms, public event pages, search results, organizers, and student submissions.
2. **Normalize:** present essential information consistently: date, deadline, mode, city, eligibility, team size, fee, theme, prizes, and official link.
3. **Clean and review:** merge duplicate listings, validate dates and links, flag uncertain records, and let people report incorrect details.
4. **Match:** let students search and filter by what they need, including city or distance, online mode, theme, deadline, team size, eligibility, and cost.
5. **Help them follow through:** save events, track applications, receive reminders, find teammates, and return to the official organizer to register.

The core promise is: **less searching, fewer missed deadlines, and a clearer path from discovery to registration.**

## Market

### Who and where

| Market layer | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Initial geography** | India, with Pune as a practical first city and online events open to participants across India. This is a recommended launch focus, based on the product plan. |
| **Primary users** | College and university students who want to build projects, learn, meet collaborators, win prizes, or strengthen their portfolio. |
| **Adjacent users** | First-time hackers, student club members, designers and other non-coding collaborators, recent graduates, and working professionals eligible for open events. |
| **Supply partners** | College clubs, universities, event organizers, developer communities, and companies running hackathons or innovation challenges. |
| **Potential paying customers** | Organizers and sponsors seeking relevant student reach, event promotion, and eventually aggregate campaign reporting. Students should be able to discover and use the core product for free. |

### Market size: use a careful proxy

India's Ministry of Education reported **4.50 crore higher-education enrolments in 2023–24**, including **1.02 crore students enrolled in STEM**. These figures establish a large potential student audience; they are **not** HackathonFinder's paying market, and they do not say how many students join hackathons or use an event-discovery product. The realistic first audience is a smaller group of digitally active students who are interested in project-building opportunities. [Ministry of Education / PIB, AISHE 2022–23 and 2023–24 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2282525&lang=1&reg=1)

Do not present the enrolment totals as a TAM or claim a revenue market size from them. A defensible market estimate needs first-party evidence: active student users by city, event volume and organizer demand, conversion to registration, and what organizers will pay for measurable reach.

### Recommended market entry

Start with **one city plus nationwide online events**, rather than claiming complete India-wide coverage on day one. Pune is the proposed first local market in the product concept. Expand to other metros and student hubs only when source coverage, event accuracy, and submissions are working reliably. This lets the product test both parts of the promise: useful nearby events and online events students can join from anywhere in India.

## Target audiences

### Primary: student builders

- **First-time participants:** want beginner-friendly events, plain-language eligibility, and confidence that an opportunity is legitimate.
- **Active student hackers:** want more events, specific themes, better deadlines and location filters, plus teammates.
- **Students with location or budget constraints:** want realistic in-person options nearby, free or low-cost events, and online alternatives.
- **Students building a portfolio:** want to track participation, projects, finalist placements, and wins.

### Secondary: organizers and ecosystem partners

- **College clubs and student organizers:** need to reach participants beyond their campus and avoid relying only on followers or group chats.
- **Companies and sponsors:** need builders who match a theme, tool, or talent audience.
- **Universities and developer communities:** can submit opportunities, share reliable feeds, and help students find collaborators.

### Who to prioritize first

Focus product decisions and outreach on **Indian college students who actively want to join a hackathon but do not already follow every organizer or platform**. Organizers are important supply partners; do not build the business around paid promotion before student traffic and organizer value are demonstrated.

## Competitive landscape

There are real alternatives. The market is not empty. HackathonFinder's opportunity is to serve a particular discovery problem better, not to claim that no one else aggregates events.

| Competitor or substitute | What it does well | Overlap with HackathonFinder | Opening for HackathonFinder |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unstop** | Large India-focused career and opportunity platform covering competitions, hackathons, jobs, and more. Events are hosted and registered on its platform. [Unstop](https://unstop.com/) | Strong direct competitor for student attention and hackathon listings in India. | Be neutral across hosting platforms; make cross-source discovery, nearby events, and consistent comparison the core job. |
| **Devfolio** | Hosts and promotes hackathons in its ecosystem and provides a discovery surface for its community. [Devfolio Discover](https://devfolio.co/discover) | Strong overlap, especially for developer and Web3 events. | Include events that are not hosted on Devfolio and provide broader city, eligibility, and deadline comparison. |
| **Devpost** | Global hackathon hosting and discovery, including many online and company-run events. [Devpost](https://devpost.com/) | Direct competition for global and online event discovery. | Prioritize India-specific eligibility, local/offline fit, and sources outside Devpost. |
| **Hack2skill** | India-based event and innovation challenge ecosystem with event pages, eligibility, timelines, and registration. [Hack2skill](https://hack2skill.com/) | Direct overlap for India-based hackathons and challenges. | Aggregate across platforms and college/community sources instead of requiring the event to be hosted in one ecosystem. |
| **HackathonRadar** | Global discovery product with search, filters, maps, alerts, event quality information, and organizer/sponsor tools. [HackathonRadar](https://www.hackathonradar.com/) | One of the closest direct aggregator competitors; its capabilities overlap with several planned HackathonFinder features. | Build a stronger India and city-level experience, especially for student eligibility, local sourcing, and teammate workflows. This differentiation must be proven in the product. |
| **HackHQ** | Community-maintained global listing with search, event status, a map, and application tracking. [HackHQ](https://github.com/Hack-HQ/hackhq) | Direct overlap in listing and discovery. | Offer more structured India-specific details, organizer and student submission paths, and a complete repeat-use workflow. |
| **Major League Hacking (MLH)** | Runs a network of developer events and publishes a season event calendar, including events in India. [MLH 2026 Events](https://www.mlh.com/seasons/2026/events) | Competes for event discovery and credibility, especially for its member events. | Cover events outside the MLH network and prioritize local, cross-platform search. |
| **HackerEarth and challenge platforms** | Offer coding challenges, assessments, and company or community competitions. [HackerEarth](https://www.hackerearth.com/challenges/) | Adjacent competition for online challenge discovery and student participation. | Make event discovery across multiple hosts and offline locations the main experience. |
| **Instagram, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Discord, college pages, and friends** | Fast, trusted within existing communities, and often where smaller events are first announced. | The biggest discovery substitutes and a source of missed or unstructured listings. | Make it easy to submit a link or poster, then turn it into a structured, searchable event with a clear source. |

### What the comparison means

- **Host platforms are not only competitors.** They are also event sources and the final registration destination. HackathonFinder should link out and send them qualified participants.
- **Other aggregators already offer maps, filters, alerts, and tracking.** Those features alone are not a durable advantage.
- **The strongest potential edge is local completeness and trust:** reliably finding relevant Indian events from formal platforms and less formal college/community sources, keeping the records accurate, and helping students decide whether an event fits.
- **The biggest risk is data quality.** A broad list with stale deadlines, missing eligibility, or weak local coverage will lose trust quickly.

The system's documented workflow normalizes event details, deduplicates records, validates listings, and sends uncertain items to review. Avoid saying that HackathonFinder currently finds *every* event or automatically reads private/community accounts. The browser extension and production alert delivery should also be confirmed before claiming availability.

### What is “good level” for this product?

- **Category:** a focused event discovery marketplace / aggregator for hackathons and related build competitions.
- **Build maturity:** the repository documents an early product with a broad feature set and real ingestion/backend foundations. This is beyond a slide-only idea, but code and documentation do not establish production reliability.
- **Competitive maturity:** differentiation is not proven. Search, filters, maps, alerts, and saved events are expected capabilities in this category.
- **Product-market fit:** not demonstrated by the repository. There is no evidence here of active users, complete event coverage, retention, organizer revenue, or registration conversion.
- **The next “good level”:** publish one dependable city/online inventory, get students using it repeatedly, measure successful clicks to official registration, and show that organizers value the audience.

This is a stage assessment from the project files, not an external product ranking.

## Positioning

**For Indian students looking for their next build opportunity, HackathonFinder is the cross-platform discovery guide that helps them compare relevant hackathons in one place. Unlike an event host that mainly lists its own registrations, HackathonFinder links opportunities from multiple sources and sends students to the official organizer to join.**

Use this promise consistently:

> **Find the right hackathon for you — near you or online — before the deadline.**

## Business model

Keep discovery free for students. Possible revenue, in order of validation:

1. **Featured event placements** with a clear sponsored label and relevance rules.
2. **Organizer promotion packages** across city pages, email digests, or social distribution.
3. **Sponsor placements** for companies seeking opt-in access to builders interested in a theme or tool.
4. **College and community partnerships** for recurring event distribution and student engagement.
5. **Opt-in talent discovery** only later, with transparent student consent and control over what is shared.

Start charging only after the product can show organizers real, relevant reach and trackable registration clicks. Never sell student personal data; the product concept already commits to using student data for personalization rather than resale.

## Next goals

### Goal 1 — Make event quality visible

- Define a minimum listing standard: working official link, open/closed status, deadline, mode, location, eligibility, and last-checked time.
- Track accuracy, duplicate rate, missing-field rate, stale-listing rate, and source failure rate by source.
- Review uncertain and social-source items before publication; make reporting and correction easy.
- Set clear coverage limits by city and event type instead of marketing unsupported “every hackathon” coverage.

### Goal 2 — Launch a narrow, useful market

- Start with Pune listings plus online events available to students across India.
- Recruit campus clubs and student ambassadors to submit events and test the search journey.
- Interview at least 20 students across first-time and experienced participants; observe their current search process and test whether the product saves time.
- Interview at least 10 organizers about participant reach, current promotion channels, and what evidence would justify paying.

### Goal 3 — Prove repeat student value

- Measure how many visitors use search/filter, open an event, save it, and click through to official registration.
- Add reminder/digest workflows that work reliably and let users control frequency.
- Test teammate matching with a small group before scaling community features.
- Publish truthful city and theme pages only where listing inventory is useful and current.

### Goal 4 — Expand and monetize based on evidence

- Add the next cities when event coverage, data quality, and community submissions meet a defined threshold.
- Add source connectors when permitted and operationally sustainable; keep a manual/community path for sources that cannot be safely automated.
- Pilot paid featured placements only after students return and organizers can see attributable clicks.
- Expand to adjacent contests and challenges only when they help the core student job rather than dilute it.

## Success metrics

| Question | Metric |
|---|---|
| Are we finding enough? | Share of known public events in the launch region that are listed; source coverage by channel. |
| Can students trust the listings? | Correct deadline/link/date rate; stale and duplicate rates; user reports resolved. |
| Is discovery faster? | Time to find a relevant event; search-to-event-detail rate; filter usage. |
| Does the product drive action? | Official registration clicks per active student and per listing. |
| Do students return? | Weekly returning users, saved events, reminder opt-ins, and digest engagement. |
| Does the supply side care? | Organizer submissions/claims, repeat organizers, qualified click-throughs, and paid pilot conversion. |

Do not optimize for raw listing count by itself. A smaller, current, useful inventory is more valuable than a large stale one.

## Main risks and response

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Stale or incorrect event information damages trust. | Validate deadlines and official links, show last-checked time, run reports, and review uncertain records. |
| Platform changes, robots rules, or API limits reduce ingestion. | Follow source terms, use official interfaces where available, monitor source health, and support submissions. |
| Large platforms and other aggregators already have audience and features. | Win a narrower Indian/local segment through coverage quality, student fit, and distribution through campus communities. |
| Too many features distract from the core job. | Prioritize reliable discovery, accurate listings, and registration clicks before expanding community and organizer products. |
| Organizer revenue arrives before audience value. | Keep early organizer tools free or low-friction; charge after proving relevant reach and attribution. |
| Listing posters, data, or user profiles create rights/privacy concerns. | Link to the official source, use concise original summaries, respect source rules, collect only needed user data, and provide correction/removal paths. |

## Pitch-ready closing

> HackathonFinder is building the discovery layer for student build opportunities in India. Students should not need to follow ten platforms and dozens of college pages to find one event they are eligible for. We bring listings together, make the details comparable, and help students act on time. Our next milestone is to prove that we can maintain a useful, accurate inventory for one city and online events, then grow through student and organizer communities.
